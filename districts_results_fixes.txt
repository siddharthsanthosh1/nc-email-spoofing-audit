DNS records to fix each unprotected domain.
Start at Step 1 (p=none). Never jump straight to p=reject on a domain
that has never had DMARC — legitimate mail from unlisted senders will bounce.

=== alleghany.k12.nc.us ===
Step 1 (today, safe, breaks nothing) — turn on monitoring:
  Host:  _dmarc.alleghany.k12.nc.us
  Type:  TXT
  Value: v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@alleghany.k12.nc.us; fo=1

Step 2 (after 2-4 weeks of reading reports, once all legitimate senders pass):
  Value: v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; pct=100; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@alleghany.k12.nc.us; fo=1

Step 3 (final, recommended by CISA for government domains):
  Value: v=DMARC1; p=reject; pct=100; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@alleghany.k12.nc.us; fo=1

If there is no SPF record, add one listing your real mail servers, for example
for Google Workspace:
  Host:  alleghany.k12.nc.us
  Type:  TXT
  Value: v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com -all
(replace the include with your actual provider — Microsoft 365 is include:spf.protection.outlook.com)
